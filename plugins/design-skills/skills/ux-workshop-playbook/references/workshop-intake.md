# Workshop Intake

Use this when a workshop request starts from management scope, vague stakeholder direction, a product goal, a messy problem, or unclear expectations.

## Intake Questions

Ask only the questions needed to shape the workshop:

- What decision, alignment, or learning must this workshop produce?
- What triggered the need for a workshop now?
- What scope has management already set?
- Which parts of the scope are fixed, flexible, or unknown?
- Who owns the final decision?
- Who has evidence, constraints, or authority?
- What happens if the workshop does not happen?
- What artifact should exist afterward?
- What prior research, recordings, metrics, support tickets, designs, or notes should inform the session?
- What timeline, tool, confidentiality, or participant constraints matter?

## Translate Management Scope

Convert management input into:

- Workshop purpose.
- Decision needed.
- Success criteria.
- Constraints and non-negotiables.
- Open questions.
- Required participants.
- Evidence to review before the session.
- Output expected by management.

## Workshop Readiness Check

Recommend a workshop when:

- The team needs shared understanding.
- Multiple functions hold necessary evidence or constraints.
- The problem is too ambiguous for async review.
- A decision requires visible tradeoffs.
- Participants need to co-create or align on ownership.

Recommend a lighter alternative when:

- One decision owner can decide after reviewing evidence.
- The ask is mainly status sharing.
- The problem is actually missing user evidence.
- Participants do not have authority or relevant knowledge.
- The desired output is already known.
