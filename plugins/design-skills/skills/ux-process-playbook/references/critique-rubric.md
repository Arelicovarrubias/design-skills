# Critique Rubric

Use this to structure design critiques, review agendas, and feedback synthesis for flows, screens, prototypes, and design-system components.

## Critique Setup

- Goal: what decision or improvement the critique should support.
- Audience: who the design serves.
- Design maturity: sketch, concept, prototype, pre-handoff, post-build QA.
- Feedback needed: flow logic, usability, content, accessibility, visual system fit, feasibility, or launch readiness.
- Feedback not needed: topics that would distract or reopen settled decisions.

## Review Lenses

Task flow:

- Is the user's next action clear?
- Does the sequence match the user's mental model?
- Are detours, exits, and recovery paths clear?

Information hierarchy:

- Can users scan the screen and understand what matters first?
- Are labels specific and plain?
- Are groupings, density, and emphasis helping comprehension?

Interaction and states:

- Are controls familiar and correctly matched to the action?
- Are loading, empty, error, disabled, success, and edge states accounted for?
- Are destructive or high-stakes actions protected?

Accessibility:

- Are contrast, target size, focus order, keyboard paths, alt text, motion, and plain language considered?
- Are errors and instructions perceivable without relying only on color?

System fit:

- Does the design reuse existing components and patterns?
- Are spacing, typography, color, icons, and responsive behavior consistent?

Implementation readiness:

- Are specs, variants, content rules, data dependencies, and acceptance criteria clear?
- Are unknowns assigned to owners?

## Critique Agenda

1. State goal, audience, maturity, and decision needed.
2. Show the flow or artifact without interruption.
3. Capture clarifying questions.
4. Review through selected lenses.
5. Separate findings into blockers, important improvements, and polish.
6. Decide what changes now, later, or not at all.
7. Assign owners and capture unresolved questions.
